# Sample AI round: 30 questions for review

Written by Claude with the quiz page's exact AI prompt (seminary level), then run through the page's checks: no repeats of earlier questions, answer-length balance, and every quotation matched word for word against the OPC Westminster Confession and Larger Catechism. Dort questions paraphrase only, because the CRCNA translation is under copyright.

In each question, **✓** marks the answer the quiz treats as correct. The page shuffles the options for the reader.

## Canons of Dort

### 1. Head I · Art. 3
**According to I.3, why does the gospel reach some peoples and times and not others?**

- ✓ God sends messengers of the good news to whom He wills and when He wills
- Some nations used the light of nature better, so God rewarded them with the gospel
- The church neglected its mission, which God permits but does not direct in any way
- God sends it to all people equally, though many refuse it before it reaches them

*Source:* Canons of Dort I.3  
*Explanation:* The Canons say that God, in mercy, sends heralds of the gospel to whom He wills and when He wills, so that people may be brought to faith. The idea that God rewards a better use of natural light with the gospel is a Remonstrant teaching that the Rejection of Errors under Heads III/IV rejects.

### 2. Head I · Art. 6
**To what does I.6 trace the fact that some receive faith in time and others do not?**

- ✓ God's eternal decree, by which He softens the hearts of the elect and leaves others in hardness
- The use of free will, since God gives every hearer the same power to believe the gospel
- The faithfulness of parents, whose covenant care God promises to bless with saving faith
- God's foreknowledge of faith, which He then confirms by a decree made later in eternity

*Source:* Canons of Dort I.6  
*Explanation:* Article 6 says the giving of faith to some and not to others comes from God's eternal decree. God graciously softens the hearts of the elect, and by a just judgment He leaves others in their wickedness. Foreseen faith as the ground of the decree is the view that I.9 rejects.

### 3. Head I · Art. 8
**What does I.8 teach about the number of God's decrees of election?**

- ✓ There is one decree of election for all who are saved in the Old and New Testaments
- There are several decrees, one for each nation that God calls to Himself in history
- There are two decrees, a general one to save believers and a special one for persons
- There is one decree for Israel and a second for the Gentiles who later join the church

*Source:* Canons of Dort I.8  
*Explanation:* Election is not of many kinds; it is one and the same for all who are saved in both Testaments, because Scripture speaks of a single good pleasure and plan of God. The idea of a general decree to save believers and a separate decree for individuals is a Remonstrant view rejected in Rejection of Errors I.2.

### 4. Head I · Art. 10
**What does I.10 name as the cause of election?**

- ✓ God's good pleasure alone, by which He chose certain persons rather than human qualities
- God's foresight of holiness, which He set as the condition for adopting persons
- God's mercy toward the least sinful, whom He judged most fit to receive grace
- God's wish to reward faith, which He chose as the one condition of salvation

*Source:* Canons of Dort I.10  
*Explanation:* The cause of election is God's good pleasure alone. It does not consist in God choosing certain human qualities or actions as a condition of salvation; He adopted certain persons out of the common mass of sinners. Holiness and faith are fruits of election, not conditions that God foresaw.

### 5. Head I · Art. 11
**How does I.11 describe the stability of God's election?**

- ✓ It cannot be interrupted or changed, so the elect cannot be cast away nor their number reduced
- It changes in time, since God chooses anew as people come to faith or fall away from it
- It is fixed for the church as a body, but not for the individual persons who belong to it
- It is certain for those who persevere, but can be revoked for those who fall into sin

*Source:* Canons of Dort I.11  
*Explanation:* Because God is supremely wise, unchangeable, and almighty, His election can be neither interrupted nor changed, recalled nor annulled. The elect cannot be cast away, and their number cannot be reduced. Election of the church only as a body, with no fixed persons, is a Remonstrant position the Synod rejected.

### 6. Head II · Art. 4
**What reasons does II.4 give for the infinite value of Christ's death?**

- ✓ The one who died is a true and holy man and the eternal Son, and He bore God's wrath and curse
- The Father agreed to count it as enough, though in itself it had only a limited worth
- The number of the elect was great, so the price had to be large enough to cover them all
- His sufferings were longer and more intense than any that another person ever endured

*Source:* Canons of Dort II.4  
*Explanation:* The death has infinite value because the person who suffered is not only a true and perfectly holy man but also the only begotten Son of God, and because His death came with the experience of God's wrath and curse. The idea that God merely agreed to accept a lesser payment is rejected in Rejection of Errors II.3.

### 7. Heads III/IV · Art. 6
**What does III/IV.6 say accomplishes what neither the light of nature nor the law can do?**

- ✓ God does it by the Holy Spirit through the gospel, the message of reconciliation
- The law does it when it is preached with power, since it awakens a desire for holiness
- Natural light does it in those who use it well, since God rewards that faithful use
- The sacraments do it, since the Spirit works through them apart from the preached word

*Source:* Canons of Dort III/IV.5-6  
*Explanation:* Articles 4-5 show that natural light and the law cannot bring saving grace. Article 6 says that God does this by the Holy Spirit through the word, the ministry of reconciliation, which is the gospel about the Messiah. The law can show sin but cannot give the strength to escape it.

### 8. Heads III/IV · Art. 15
**How should believers regard people who are not yet called, according to III/IV.15?**

- ✓ They should pray for them, and not act proudly as if they had made themselves differ
- They should avoid them, since contact with the uncalled endangers the grace of the elect
- They should count them reprobate, since the lack of a call reveals God's secret decree
- They should press them with arguments, since grace waits for the right reasons to act

*Source:* Canons of Dort III/IV.15  
*Explanation:* God owes this grace to no one, so believers have nothing to boast of. The article tells them to pray for those not yet called, as for those who do not yet exist as believers, and not to treat them haughtily. Concluding that someone is reprobate goes beyond what the article permits.

### 9. Head V · Art. 7
**According to V.7, how does God restore believers after a serious fall?**

- ✓ He keeps in them the imperishable seed and renews them by His word and Spirit to repentance
- The church restores them through penance, which God accepts as satisfaction for the sin
- They restore themselves by their remaining strength, which grace never fully removes
- God gives them a second new birth, since the first regeneration was lost in the fall

*Source:* Canons of Dort V.7  
*Explanation:* God preserves in His fallen saints the imperishable seed from which they were born again, so that it does not perish. Then by His word and Spirit He renews them to repentance, so that they seek pardon in Christ and again experience His grace. The Canons do not teach that regeneration is lost and repeated.

### 10. Head V · Art. 14
**Through what means does God carry on His work of grace in believers, according to V.14?**

- ✓ Through hearing and reading the gospel, meditation, warnings, promises and the sacraments
- Through an inward work alone, since outward means play no role in keeping His people
- Through the believer's vows, which bind the will to stay faithful until the very end
- Through visions and private words, which confirm the elect when they are tempted

*Source:* Canons of Dort V.14  
*Explanation:* Just as God began His work of grace through the preaching of the gospel, He preserves, continues and completes it by hearing and reading the gospel, by meditation on it, by its exhortations, threats and promises, and by the use of the sacraments. Perseverance therefore does not make the means unnecessary.

## Westminster Confession

### 11. Ch. 1 · Scripture
**According to WCF 1.10, who is the supreme judge in controversies of religion?**

- ✓ The Holy Spirit speaking in the Scripture, to whose sentence councils and opinions must yield
- A general council lawfully called, whose decrees the Spirit guards from error in faith
- The agreement of the church fathers, which shows how Scripture was always understood
- The reason of each believer, enlightened by the Spirit to judge what agrees with Scripture

*Source:* WCF 1.10  
*Explanation:* The supreme judge "can be no other but the Holy Spirit speaking in the Scripture." Decrees of councils, opinions of ancient writers, and doctrines of men are all examined by that judge. Councils and fathers are tested by Scripture, so they cannot be the final judge.

### 12. Ch. 5 · Providence
**How does WCF 5.4 relate God's providence to the sins of angels and men?**

- ✓ It reaches even sin, and not by bare permission, yet sin's sinfulness comes only from the creature
- It reaches sin by bare permission only, since God stands aside and does not order sinful acts
- It reaches sin as an active cause, so God and the creature share the sinfulness of each act
- It reaches sin by foreknowledge alone, since God sees sin coming but does not govern it

*Source:* WCF 5.4  
*Explanation:* Providence extends to the first fall and all other sins, "and that not by a bare permission," but joined with a wise and powerful bounding and ordering of them. Yet "the sinfulness thereof proceedeth only from the creature, and not from God." So God governs sin without being its author.

### 13. Ch. 6 · Fall and Sin
**What does WCF 6.5 say about the corruption of nature in those who are regenerate?**

- ✓ It remains in this life, and though pardoned and mortified, it and its motions are truly sin
- It is removed at baptism, and what remains afterward is a weakness that is not properly sin
- It remains only in the body, while the soul is renewed completely at the moment of conversion
- It remains, but its motions are not sin until the will freely consents to act on them

*Source:* WCF 6.5  
*Explanation:* This corruption "doth remain in those that are regenerated," and although it is pardoned and mortified through Christ, "yet both itself, and all the motions thereof, are truly and properly sin." The view that desire is not sin until the will consents is the Roman Catholic teaching on concupiscence.

### 14. Ch. 12 · Adoption
**Which privilege of adoption does WCF 12.1 include?**

- ✓ God pities, protects and chastens them as a father, yet they are never cast off
- God frees them from all fatherly correction, since chastening belongs to the law
- God adopts them by baptism, and may disown them if they fall into grave sin
- God makes them heirs, but gives them the Spirit of adoption only at their death

*Source:* WCF 12.1  
*Explanation:* The adopted are "pitied, protected, provided for, and chastened by him, as by a Father: yet never cast off, but sealed to the day of redemption." Chastening is one of the privileges of sonship, not a sign that adoption is lost. The Spirit of adoption is given in this life, not only at death.

### 15. Ch. 13 · Sanctification
**How does WCF 13.2 describe the extent of sanctification in this life?**

- ✓ It reaches the whole person, yet stays imperfect, so a continual war remains within
- It is complete in some believers, who reach a state free from all known sin
- It reaches the will and affections, but leaves the mind as it was before
- It renews the outward life first, and reaches the heart only near the end

*Source:* WCF 13.2  
*Explanation:* Sanctification is "throughout, in the whole man; yet imperfect in this life," because some remnants of corruption remain in every part. From this arises "a continual and irreconcilable war," the flesh lusting against the Spirit. The perfectionist claim of a sinless state contradicts this section.

### 16. Ch. 14 · Saving Faith
**What does WCF 14.2 call the principal acts of saving faith?**

- ✓ Accepting, receiving and resting upon Christ alone for justification, sanctification and eternal life
- Assenting to the creeds of the church and obeying its teaching in every matter of faith and life
- Feeling assurance of one's own election and of the full pardon of all one's past and present sins
- Promising obedience to the law, which God then accepts as the condition of everlasting life

*Source:* WCF 14.2  
*Explanation:* Faith believes whatever God reveals in His word, but "the principal acts of saving faith are accepting, receiving, and resting upon Christ alone for justification, sanctification, and eternal life." Assurance is not of the essence of faith (18.3), so it is not one of these principal acts.

### 17. Ch. 15 · Repentance
**How does WCF 15.3 relate repentance to the pardon of sin?**

- ✓ It is no satisfaction for sin, yet none may expect pardon without it
- It is a partial satisfaction, which God accepts together with the merit of Christ
- It is unnecessary for the elect, since God decreed their pardon in eternity
- It is required once at conversion, but not for the sins a person commits after

*Source:* WCF 15.3  
*Explanation:* Repentance is not to be rested in "as any satisfaction for sin, or any cause of the pardon thereof," which is God's free grace in Christ. Yet it "is of such necessity to all sinners, that none may expect pardon without it." WCF 15.5 adds that people should repent of particular sins particularly, so repentance continues after conversion.

### 18. Ch. 23 · Civil Magistrate
**What does WCF 23.3, in the American text, say civil magistrates may not do?**

- ✓ Administer the word and sacraments or the keys, though they must protect the church
- Use discipline in the church when its own officers fail to act against public offenses
- Show any care for the church, since church and state must stay wholly separate
- Appoint ministers to churches, though they may not themselves preach or baptize

*Source:* WCF 23.3  
*Explanation:* Civil magistrates "may not assume to themselves the administration of the Word and sacraments; or the power of the keys of the kingdom of heaven." Yet it is their duty, as nursing fathers, to protect the church without preferring any denomination. This wording comes from the American revision of 1788.

### 19. Ch. 25 · The Church
**Whom does WCF 25.6 name as the head of the church?**

- ✓ The Lord Jesus Christ alone, so the bishop of Rome cannot be its head
- Christ in heaven, with the bishop of Rome as His visible vicar on earth
- The general council, which represents the whole church on earth under Christ
- The civil ruler, who governs the church in its outward affairs under Christ

*Source:* WCF 25.6  
*Explanation:* "There is no other head of the Church but the Lord Jesus Christ." The section adds that the Pope of Rome cannot in any sense be head of it. A council or a civil ruler may serve the church, but neither is its head.

### 20. Ch. 33 · Last Judgment
**Why does WCF 33.3 say Christ keeps the day of judgment unknown?**

- ✓ So that people may shake off carnal security and always be watchful, not knowing the hour
- So that the church cannot set dates, which would lead to division among believers
- So that the wicked cannot repent at the last moment and so escape the judgment
- So that believers will rely on signs in the world, which announce the coming day

*Source:* WCF 33.3  
*Explanation:* Christ would have all certainly persuaded that there shall be a judgment, but He keeps the day unknown "that they may shake off all carnal security, and be always watchful, because they know not at what hour the Lord will come." The section points believers to readiness, not to reading signs.

## Reformed Systematics

### 21. Prolegomena
**By what two means, according to Belgic Confession 2, do we know God?**

- ✓ By the creation, preservation and government of the world, and by His holy and divine word
- By reason and conscience, which together lead every person to a saving knowledge of God
- By Scripture and tradition, which together form one deposit of divine revelation for the church
- By the inner light and the written word, which agree with each other in every true believer

*Source:* Belgic Confession 2  
*Explanation:* Belgic 2 calls the universe a beautiful book in which all creatures point to God, and then says God makes Himself known more clearly by His word, as far as we need for His glory and our salvation. General revelation leaves people without excuse but does not save; tradition is not a second source of revelation.

### 22. Prolegomena
**What does WCF 1.7 claim about the clarity of Scripture?**

- ✓ What is needed for salvation is clear enough that the unlearned, using ordinary means, may understand it
- All passages are equally clear, so no part of Scripture ever needs a teacher's help to explain it
- Only the learned can understand it, so the church must interpret it for all the other people
- It is clear to those with the Spirit, so ordinary means such as study and preaching are not needed

*Source:* WCF 1.7  
*Explanation:* Not all things in Scripture are equally plain, yet what is necessary for salvation is so clearly set out "that not only the learned, but the unlearned, in a due use of the ordinary means, may attain unto a sufficient understanding of them." Perspicuity does not remove the need for means such as preaching and study.

### 23. Doctrine of God
**Reformed theologians distinguish God's decretive will from His preceptive will. What is the preceptive will?**

- ✓ What God commands as our duty, which people can and often do break
- What God has decreed will happen, which no creature is able to resist
- What God permits to happen, which He neither commands nor decrees
- What God desires in Himself, apart from His commands and His decree

*Source:* Louis Berkhof, Systematic Theology, Part I, "The Will of God"; Deuteronomy 29:29  
*Explanation:* The decretive (secret) will determines what will come to pass and always takes effect. The preceptive (revealed) will is the rule of our duty, given in the law and the gospel, and people often transgress it. Permission falls under the decretive will in Reformed theology, so it is not a third, separate will.

### 24. Anthropology
**Which view of the origin of each human soul did most Reformed theologians hold?**

- ✓ Creationism: God creates each soul directly and joins it to the body from the parents
- Traducianism: the whole person, soul and body alike, comes from the parents by generation
- Pre-existence: souls were made before the world, and each one later enters a body
- Emanation: the soul flows out of God's own being, as a spark of the divine nature

*Source:* Louis Berkhof, Systematic Theology, Part II, "The Origin of the Soul"  
*Explanation:* Most Reformed theologians, with Calvin, Turretin and Berkhof, held creationism: God creates each soul immediately. Traducianism was the common Lutheran view, and some Reformed writers such as Shedd held it too, so it is a minority view among the Reformed, not a heresy. Pre-existence came from Origen and was rejected by the church.

### 25. Christology
**How does WCF 8.7 explain the communication of properties in Christ?**

- ✓ What belongs to one nature is sometimes attributed to the person named by the other nature
- Attributes of the divine nature pass into the human nature, so that the flesh shares them
- The two natures trade their attributes, so each nature receives the other's properties
- The person acts through the divine nature only, while the human nature stays passive

*Source:* WCF 8.7  
*Explanation:* Christ acts according to both natures, each doing what is proper to itself; yet by reason of the unity of the person, "that which is proper to one nature is sometimes in Scripture attributed to the person denominated by the other nature." Acts 20:28 speaks of God's own blood. The transfer of divine attributes to the flesh is the Lutheran position.

### 26. Christology
**Why, according to WLC 42, is our Mediator called Christ?**

- ✓ He was anointed with the Spirit above measure to be prophet, priest and king of His church
- He came from the line of David, whose royal house God promised to keep forever on the throne
- He was baptized in the Jordan, which began His work as Mediator and His public ministry
- He was the firstborn son of Mary, and so held the rights that belong to the firstborn

*Source:* WLC 42  
*Explanation:* Our Mediator was called Christ "because he was anointed with the Holy Ghost above measure" and so set apart and equipped to carry out the offices of prophet, priest, and king in both His humiliation and exaltation. His Davidic descent is true, but WLC 42 grounds the name in the anointing.

### 27. Soteriology
**How does WCF 10.2 describe the sinner in effectual calling?**

- ✓ Wholly passive, until the Spirit quickens and renews him so that he can answer the call
- Cooperating, since grace offers help and the will joins with it to produce new life
- Active first, since the sinner must turn to God before the Spirit renews the heart
- Passive in baptism, but active and cooperating in every later stage of conversion

*Source:* WCF 10.2  
*Explanation:* Effectual calling comes from God's free grace alone, not from anything foreseen in man, "who is altogether passive therein, until, being quickened and renewed by the Holy Spirit, he is thereby enabled to answer this call." Cooperation in the first act of renewal is the synergist view the Confession excludes.

### 28. Soteriology
**On what does WCF 17.2 rest the perseverance of the saints?**

- ✓ Not on their free will, but on God's unchangeable election and Christ's merit and intercession
- On the believer's faithful use of the means, which God rewards with grace at the end
- On the strength of the new nature, which cannot sin at all once it is born of God
- On the prayers of the church, which keep its members from falling away from grace

*Source:* WCF 17.2  
*Explanation:* Perseverance depends "not upon their own free will, but upon the immutability of the decree of election," on the merit and intercession of Christ, the abiding of the Spirit, and the nature of the covenant of grace. WCF 17.3 says believers can still fall into grievous sins, so the new nature is not sinless.

### 29. Ecclesiology
**What does WCF 27.3 say the grace of the sacraments depends on?**

- ✓ Not on any power in them or the minister's intention, but on the Spirit and the word of institution
- On the act itself, so the sacrament gives grace whenever the receiver puts no obstacle in the way
- On the faith and right intention of the minister, which must be present at each administration
- On the receiver's faith alone, since the sacrament is a bare sign with no promise attached

*Source:* WCF 27.3  
*Explanation:* Grace is not conferred "by any power in them; neither doth the efficacy of a sacrament depend upon the piety or intention of him that doth administer it: but upon the work of the Spirit, and the word of institution." The first wrong option is the Roman ex opere operato view; the last is bare memorialism.

### 30. Eschatology
**How does WLC 87 describe the bodies of the just at the resurrection?**

- ✓ Raised by Christ's Spirit in power, spiritual and incorruptible, and made like His glorious body
- Raised as new bodies, with no link to the bodies that were laid in the grave at death
- Raised as spirits only, since the body has no lasting place in the final state of glory
- Raised as they were at death, to be changed later during a reign of a thousand years

*Source:* WLC 87  
*Explanation:* The same bodies of the dead are raised. The bodies of the just, by the Spirit of Christ and by virtue of His resurrection, are "raised in power, spiritual, incorruptible, and made like to his glorious body." The Catechism keeps continuity with the body that died, so a wholly new body is wrong.
